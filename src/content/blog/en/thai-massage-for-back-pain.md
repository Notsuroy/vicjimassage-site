Seven out of ten people who walk into my studio say the same thing, with small variations:

"I have lower back pain that keeps coming back. I've done physical therapy. I've taken medication. It improves a little, then it returns."

This is by far one of the most common reasons people come to me. And no wonder. Lower back pain is, according to the World Health Organization, **the leading cause of disability in the world today**.

This piece is about why that happens, and why traditional Thai Massage can touch that pain in a way other approaches often can't.

## Why the lower back accumulates so much

The lower back is a transition zone. It connects the upper body (which carries weight, posture, arms) with the lower body (which supports, walks, lifts). Every force that runs through the body passes through there.

Add the modern lifestyle on top of that:

- Hours sitting in front of a screen, shortening the hip flexors
- Short, high breathing patterns (chest), without mobilizing the diaphragm
- Chronic stress, which keeps the hips in "defense mode"
- Lack of varied movement, especially rotation and stretching

The result: tension accumulates in the deep muscles of the hips and lower back, especially in the **psoas**, the **quadratus lumborum**, and the **glutes**. These muscles pull the spine in directions it wasn't designed to go. Pain shows up as the symptom, but the origin is somewhere else.

## Why treating only the painful spot rarely works

The first thing most people do when they have lower back pain is massage or stretch the lower back.

It makes sense intuitively. But it usually doesn't work.

Lower back pain almost never originates only in the lower back. It's the result of tension accumulated **elsewhere**: hips, glutes, hamstrings, diaphragm, even the feet. When those muscles are short or locked, they pull the pelvis, which pulls the spine, which produces the pain.

Local massage might provide a few hours of relief. But if the cause is above or below, the pain comes back.

## How Thai Massage works differently

Traditional Thai Massage was developed with an integrated view of the body. It's not a technique that treats isolated points. It moves along **energy lines (sen)** that run through the entire body.

For lower back pain, this means the work begins **far** from the lower back.

In a typical session for someone with lower back pain, I:

1. **Start at the feet.** Yes, feet. The lines that support posture begin there. Releasing tension in the feet changes how the entire leg organizes itself.
2. **Move up through the legs, working hamstrings and glutes.** When these are short, they hold the pelvis in anterior rotation, overloading the lower back.
3. **Open the hips with passive stretches.** This is the heart of the work. Postures similar to yoga's "pigeon," but I hold them for you, adjusting depth as the body allows.
4. **Work the psoas.** The psoas connects the lumbar spine to the inner thigh. When chronically tense, it literally pulls the spine forward, creating compression in the lower back. Releasing the psoas is often the key that unlocks everything.
5. **Only then arrive at the lower back itself.** Conscious pressure along the lines parallel to the spine, spinal lengthening, breath work.
6. **Close with inversions and decompressions.** Postures where the hips are higher than the head, letting the spine decompress.

This work takes time. That's why my sessions are long (minimum 1h30, ideally 2h or more). You can't do this circuit in 30 minutes. And that's why express sessions rarely resolve chronic lower back pain.

## What to expect right after the session

Every body responds differently, but some patterns are common:

- **Immediate sensation of lightness** in the hips and lower back
- **Walking differently** as you leave (more fluid, less stiff)
- **Drowsiness**: the nervous system shifts into deep rest mode, and most people sleep exceptionally well the following night
- In some cases, **mild soreness the next day**, similar to post-workout. That's normal. It means the tissue was mobilized.

## What to expect over the following days

Most people report significant reduction in lower back pain right after the first session. But it's important to set realistic expectations:

- **If the pain is new (weeks or a few months):** one to three sessions usually resolve it.
- **If the pain is chronic (years):** this is months of work, not a single session. The body learned a pattern and will need time to reorganize. I recommend starting with weekly or every-other-week sessions for two months, then spacing out.
- **If the pain is structural in origin (confirmed hernia, severe scoliosis):** massage helps a lot as support, but should be combined with care from a physical therapist or orthopedic doctor.

I don't replace a doctor. If you have lower back pain that radiates into the leg, numbness, weakness, or pain that appeared suddenly without clear cause, **get medical evaluation first**. Massage comes after, as part of the treatment.

## Who benefits most

Patterns that tend to respond well:

- Lower back pain of postural origin (too much sitting, poor posture)
- Lower back pain related to stress and anxiety (body in constant tension)
- Lower back pain in people who train but don't recover (lifting, running)
- Postpartum lower back pain (after the recovery period)
- Chronic lower back pain without a clear diagnosis
- Travel/nomad lower back pain (long flights, sleeping in different beds, carrying a backpack daily)
